A fit that converges to a number and reports a standard error is making two claims
at once: that the estimate is near the truth, and that the reported width describes
the spread of estimates the procedure would produce. The first is what a
practitioner usually checks. The second is the one that decides whether a
confidence interval means anything, and it is not visible from a single fit.

For Hawkes processes the distinction has teeth. When the branching ratio approaches
one, the excitation and decay parameters trade off against each other along a ridge
in the likelihood, and a maximum-likelihood fit can converge to a point that looks
precise and is not. The curvature of the likelihood at that point, which is what an
inverse-Hessian standard error reads, can be steep along a direction the sampling
distribution does not respect.

This paper measures that discrepancy instead of assuming it away. It reports a
pre-registered simulation study of \HsixCells{} parameter regimes in which the ratio
of reported to realised standard error is estimated from \HsixReplications{}
replications per cell, identifies the regimes where the estimator does not exist,
and records which of six hypotheses survived. It then does the thing that the
measurement implies and most libraries do not: it makes the estimator consult its
own calibration at run time and change the interval it reports.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Three, and only three.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A public implementation.} A compiled likelihood and gradient for the
multivariate exponential-kernel Hawkes process, a maximum-likelihood fit with
observed-information standard errors, two independent exact simulators, and
time-rescaling diagnostics, released as an installable package with a pinned public
surface and a test suite that pins every figure in its documentation to a committed
output.
\item \textbf{A pre-registered calibration study with refutations.} A design frozen
before any measurement, with six dated amendments that each state what was known
when it was written; a grid of \HsixCells{} cells at \HsixReplications{}
replications; and a result in which the headline is a measured shortfall and two of
the six hypotheses are not confirmed. The refutations are reported for the same
reason as the confirmation.
\item \textbf{A shipped lookup that refuses to extrapolate.} A map of the measured
quantities, installed with the package, which answers for a regime only inside the
box it measured, says so when it has no measurement, and says the estimator is
undefined when the cell it found is one where it does not exist.
\end{enumerate}
