A fit that converges to a number and reports a standard error is making two claims
at once: that the estimate is near the truth, and that the reported width describes
the spread of estimates the procedure would produce. The first is what a
practitioner usually checks. The second is the one that decides whether a
confidence interval means anything, and it is not visible from a single fit.

For Hawkes processes the distinction has teeth. When the branching ratio approaches
one, the excitation and decay parameters trade off against each other along a ridge
in the likelihood, and a maximum-likelihood fit can converge to a point that looks
precise and is not. The curvature of the likelihood at that point, which is what an
inverse-Hessian standard error reads, can be steep along a direction the sampling
distribution does not respect. A simulation study that reports the fitted parameters
and their intervals cannot reveal that, because a single dataset carries one point
on the ridge and one curvature; the discrepancy is a property of the procedure and
is only visible when the same procedure is run many times on data generated from a
known truth.

This paper measures that discrepancy instead of assuming it away, explains where it
comes from, and then acts on the measurement. It makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A public implementation: a compiled likelihood and gradient for the
multivariate exponential-kernel Hawkes process, a maximum-likelihood fit with
observed-information standard errors, two independent exact simulators and
time-rescaling diagnostics, released as an installable package whose documented
figures are each checked against a committed output.
\item A pre-registered calibration study with two failures. The design was frozen
before any measurement and amended six times, each amendment stating what was known
when it was written. The grid has \HsixCells{} cells at \HsixReplications{}
replications. The headline is a measured shortfall, and two of the six hypotheses
are not confirmed. Those two are reported on the same page as the four that held,
with the numbers that decided them.
\item An explanation of the shortfall that stops where it stops. The asymptotic
covariance of the estimator predicts that the reported interval is correct in the
limit, and Section~\ref{sec:theory} says what has to fail for it not to be. A Jensen
correction on the reported variance gives the sign and the ordering across parameter
blocks, and it misses the magnitude in the decay block by a factor the paper
states. A second pre-registered study then rules out the remedy the derivation
suggests. The part of the derivation that could not be closed is named.
\item A shipped lookup that refuses to extrapolate. The map of measured quantities
is installed with the package. It answers for a regime only inside the box it
measured, says so when it has no measurement, and reports the estimator as
undefined where the cell it found is one in which it does not exist.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper follows that order. Section~\ref{sec:model} writes the model
and the estimator down. Section~\ref{sec:theory} sets up the asymptotic covariance
and derives the correction. Section~\ref{sec:design} gives the design and the
estimands. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the measurement, including the heavy
tail in the decay estimate and what the conditioning verdict does not see.
Section~\ref{sec:auto} reports the decision rule that acts on the measurement and
the study that tested it. Section~\ref{sec:related} places the work against the
literature, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} draws out what a practitioner should do
with it, and Section~\ref{sec:repro} states how to reproduce every number.
