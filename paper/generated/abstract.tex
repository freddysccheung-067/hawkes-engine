A fit that converges and reports a standard error makes a claim about its own
reliability that one dataset cannot check. This paper tests that claim for
maximum-likelihood fits of multivariate exponential-kernel Hawkes processes, over a
pre-registered grid of \HsixCells{} cells with \HsixReplications{} simulated
replications each. The inverse-Hessian standard errors understate sampling spread:
the ratio of mean reported standard error to empirical standard deviation, pooled
over twelve two-type cells with at least 2000 events, is \HsixQ{} $\pm$ \HsixQSe{},
and a four-type satellite agrees in direction (\HsixSatQ{} $\pm$ \HsixSatSe{}). The
shortfall is concentrated in the tail of the decay estimate, and a sandwich
covariance, the natural remedy, cannot close it because the information equality
holds. Two of six hypotheses were not confirmed: coverage rose before it fell toward
criticality, and timestamp quantisation biased the decay estimate down rather than
up. The estimator is undefined in \HsixUndefined{} of \HsixCells{} cells, where the
window holds too few decay times to identify the decay rate. The accompanying
library reports no interval there and, where its calibration map measures a
shortfall, switches to a parametric bootstrap, which a second pre-registered study
finds closer to nominal coverage.
